Build Plan
Medicnest's prototype is built (Flutter Android app in Google Play closed testing, Laravel back end on a Jaspa-hosted VPS). This plan takes it from today's build to a landing page and waiting list, then a Proof of Value (PoV) app with a small group of real users, then a safe MVP for a paid pilot, then adds features one release at a time. Step 1 is an audit of the real code against the documents, because documents drift from code. Every stage ends with a go/no-go based on numbers agreed before the work starts. After the MVP, every release follows the same loop: one main feature, agree the number it should move, build, staged rollout, measure for 2 to 4 weeks, then keep, improve or remove. Fixes come before features. Patient-facing AI (Health Assistant and prescription decoder) stays in closed testing until the regulatory review in step 10 says it may go public.

 1. Audit (no product code) - read the existing Flutter and Laravel code and produce a feature matrix: every feature in the project plan marked Built, Partial, Not built or Mismatch, with file paths as evidence; confirm the database engine, how notes and images are stored, how sign-in works, which secrets are in the repository, what analytics exist and what test data is in production. The CEO approves the matrix before step 2
 2. Landing page and waiting list - publish the one-page site with the practitioner and patient paths; waiting-list API endpoint (POST /api/v1/waitlist) with validation, rate limit, duplicate check by email or WhatsApp, POPIA consent stored with the wording version; GA4 events page_view, path_selected, form_started, waitlist_joined (role), chat_opened, chat_message_sent; UTM source stored with each sign-up; chat endpoint (POST /api/v1/site-chat) that answers product questions only with a fixed system prompt and refuses medical questions; weekly export of the waiting list for the Brand and Partnerships Manager
 3. Secrets and access - move every key (AI, Google Maps, OneSignal, Mailgun, SMS, database) into the server environment; rotate any key found in git history; Medicnest superadmin and a separate Jaspa support login; two-factor sign-in for admin and practitioner accounts
 4. PoV: doctor handwriting to approved note - stylus capture on Android tablets (strokes and image); AI pipeline: handwriting image to text, then structuring into SOAP sections with each line linked to the part of the handwriting it came from; flags for unclear words, medicine names not on the reference list, doses without units, unusual numbers and negations; side-by-side review; edit; approve with attestation; approved version locked with author, time, model and prompt version; manual typing path always available
 5. PoV: documents - referral letter and prescription draft generated only from the approved note; PDF with practice letterhead, practitioner name and HPCSA number; prescription draft prints "Requires prescriber's handwritten signature" and is never sent electronically; share the referral PDF by secure link
 6. PoV: prescription decoder (patient) - camera capture with edge detection and quality check (blur, glare, cut-off); AI reads the script into structured lines (medicine, strength, form, dose, frequency, duration, prescriber, date); each medicine matched to the medicine reference list; confidence marker per line; low-confidence and unmatched lines shown as "Could not read clearly"; plain-language "commonly used for" text from the reference list, not invented by the model; "Confirm with your pharmacist or doctor" on every result; save to My medicines as patient-scanned; image deleted after decoding unless the patient saves it
 7. PoV: Health Assistant (patient) - consent screen (AI processing of health information, voice and photos); emergency check first (rule-based red flags before any AI answer: chest pain, difficulty breathing, signs of stroke, severe bleeding, loss of consciousness, thoughts of self-harm, severe allergic reaction, pregnancy bleeding, high fever in an infant); input by text, voice note (speech-to-text, transcript shown) or photo; the model asks up to five follow-up questions, then returns a structured answer: urgency level, self-care advice from an approved advice library, what to tell the doctor, warning signs to watch; output checked by rules (no diagnosis wording, no medicine doses, no "you do not need a doctor" for anything above self-care); every answer ends with Find a GP and emergency numbers; conversations flagged for review when urgency is high or the model's confidence is low
 8. PoV: Find a GP - location permission with manual suburb fallback; Medicnest-registered GPs first (badge "On Medicnest"), then Google Places nearby results (type doctor, filtered to general practice where possible) with Google attribution; distance, open now, call, directions; only place IDs cached, per Google's terms; Places usage capped per user per day
 9. PoV release and measurement - admin verification queue and AI review queue; analytics events for the PoV metrics; closed testing on Google Play with 10 to 20 GPs and 50 to 100 patients from the waiting list for 6 weeks; weekly safety review by the clinical adviser; go/no-go metrics: handwriting word accuracy at least 95% before correction, 100% of seeded dose and unit errors flagged, doctors' review-and-approve time under 90 seconds per note, prescription decoding correct on at least 90% of lines with every low-confidence line flagged, no Health Assistant answer under-triaging a red-flag test case, at least 60% of tested GPs saying they would pay, at least 40% of patient testers using Find a GP after a Health Assistant answer
10. Regulatory, privacy and clinical safety gate - written regulatory opinion on the Health Assistant and prescription decoder (SAHPRA AI/ML medical device guidance, HPCSA AI guidelines Booklet 20); clinical safety case and hazard log signed off; POPIA documents (privacy notice, consent wording, operator agreements, cross-border record, information officer); penetration test; decide which patient features go public and in what form
11. MVP: practices, patients and records - practice accounts and roles; practitioner verification; patient records with two-identifier identity check and duplicate review queue; encounters, versions, amendments, audit log on every read and change
12. MVP: referral network - directory of participating practices and services; required-information checklist per service; referral states (draft, submitted, received, under review, more information requested, accepted, booking pending, confirmed, attended, feedback returned, closed, plus declined, redirected, cancelled, patient unreachable, did not attend); only the receiving practice can confirm an appointment; feedback filed into the GP's record; overdue timers; status and next owner always visible
13. MVP: patient app linked to practices - patient invites from the practice; appointment details and instructions; shared summaries only with the patient's consent; My medicines combining doctor-entered and scanned items with their source shown; Health Assistant and Find a GP (form decided at step 10)
14. MVP: notifications, billing and exports - OneSignal push, Mailgun email, minimal-content SMS; GP subscription billing; patient export; practice export; consent changes and withdrawal
15. Clean production and release readiness - clear test data; nightly encrypted backups with one tested restore; full regression on real Android phones and tablets; store listing, content rating and Data safety answers matched to what the code collects; staged rollout to pilot practices (20%, then 100%)
16. Release 1.1 - doctor voice recognition alongside handwriting (clinician dictation of short notes, patient consent, same approval gate), so doctors spend less of the consultation writing
17. Release 1.2 - booking through Medicnest with practices that publish availability; reminders; did-not-attend tracking
18. Release 1.3 - WhatsApp Business notifications and Health Assistant entry through WhatsApp (if the regulatory gate allows)
19. Release 2.0 - iPhone and iPad apps; legally valid electronic prescriptions only once an advanced electronic signature and pharmacy acceptance are in place; integrations with practice-management systems

Deployment notes (VPS + Laravel + Flutter)
Decisions settled up front so the PoV and MVP are not reworked later:

The API, database, files and AI gateway stay on Medicnest's dedicated Linux VPS hosted by Jaspa Hosting; Nginx in front, PHP-FPM, Laravel queues under Supervisor, scheduler by cron; restart on failure and on reboot
Anything clinical, consent-related or billable lives on the API; the app never talks to an AI provider, Google Places or a messaging provider directly with Medicnest's keys
AI calls go through one AI gateway service in the API that adds the versioned prompt, strips identifiers that are not needed, enforces timeouts and validates the output against a schema before anything reaches the user
Patient-facing AI output always passes the rule-based safety layer (red flags, banned wording, dose filter) after the model and before the user
Secrets never live in the repository or in a document; keys sit in the server environment; passwords are handed over through a password manager
Production holds no test data from launch day; testing happens on staging or Play test tracks; staging has its own database and keys
Database not exposed to the internet; least-privilege database user; firewall with only 22, 80 and 443 open; SSH keys only; fail2ban; unattended security updates
Files (handwriting images, documents, voice notes, photos) encrypted at rest and served only through signed, short-lived URLs after a permission check
Backups: nightly encrypted database and file backup off the server, a tested restore before go-live and a quarterly restore drill
SSL auto-renews; a monthly manual check
Android releases always go through a Play test track first, then a staged rollout
Every AI-generated output that a clinician approves records the model and prompt version; every patient-facing AI answer is stored with its safety-check result for review
Each release ships only when its regression checklist passes on a real device, its analytics event is confirmed and the clinical adviser has signed off any change to AI prompts or safety rules

Later (not v1)
Flagged explicitly so they don't get built by accident while someone's "in there":

Diagnosis or named-condition output in the Health Assistant (only if the regulatory review allows, with clinical validation)
Medicine dose advice or changes to prescriptions from the Health Assistant or prescription decoder
Ambient recording of whole consultations
Video consultations (telehealth)
Electronic prescriptions sent to pharmacies
Medical-scheme claims and pre-authorisation
Integration with national systems (NHI, HPRS) until access is authorised
Paid placement in Find a GP that is not clearly labelled
Selling or sharing de-identified data with third parties
Public-sector rollout
